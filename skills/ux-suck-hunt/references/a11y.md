# Accessibility

Covers WCAG 2.2 A and AA success criteria (SC), ARIA keyboard patterns, screen reader checks, cognitive accessibility, and applying WCAG to native and mobile software. Read when: the product has any UI; the target is WCAG 2.2 AA. For target-size depth see touch.md; for colour and type design see visual.md. axe rule ids come from axe-core [4]; axe: none means only a human check works, and a passing axe run never proves an SC is met.
Summarised in our own words from the sources at the end; numbers and names are cited as facts.

## Perceivable (WCAG 2.2 principle 1)

- **A11Y-1** SC 1.1.1 (A): Every image, icon and other non-text item needs a text equivalent that does the same job; purely decorative ones get `alt=""` so screen readers skip them.
  Check: axe `image-alt`, `input-image-alt`, `svg-img-alt`, `role-img-alt`, `area-alt`, `object-alt`; then judge by hand whether each alt says what the image is for. [1][4]
- **A11Y-2** SC 1.2.1 (A): Recorded audio with no video needs a transcript; recorded video with no sound needs a transcript or a narration track.
  Check: axe none; look beside each media file for the transcript. [1]
- **A11Y-3** SC 1.2.2 (A): Recorded video with sound carries captions.
  Check: axe `video-caption` (needs review); look for `<track kind="captions">` or open captions in the picture. [1][4]
- **A11Y-4** SC 1.2.3 (A): Recorded video carries audio description or a full text alternative of what is shown.
  Check: axe none; list every recorded video; one with no described version, no `<track kind="descriptions">` and no linked text alternative that covers what is shown fails. [1]
- **A11Y-5** SC 1.2.4 (AA): Live streams with sound carry captions.
  Check: axe none; list live streams with sound; one with no real-time captions (a caption control or burned-in text) fails. [1]
- **A11Y-6** SC 1.2.5 (AA): Recorded video carries audio description (a text alternative alone no longer passes at AA).
  Check: axe none; list every recorded video; one whose soundtrack leaves visual information unspoken, with no described version or `<track kind="descriptions">`, fails. [1]
- **A11Y-7** SC 1.3.1 (A): Whatever structure the eye sees (headings, lists, tables, grouped fields, labels) must also exist in the markup or in text.
  Check: axe `list`, `listitem`, `definition-list`, `td-headers-attr`, `th-has-data-cells`, `aria-required-children`, `aria-required-parent`; search for styled `<div>` or `<p>` posing as headings (axe `p-as-heading`, experimental). [1][4]
- **A11Y-8** SC 1.3.2 (A): When order carries meaning, the source and accessibility-tree order must follow the visual reading order.
  Check: axe none; disable CSS or walk the page with a screen reader and compare; search for `order:`, `flex-direction: *-reverse` and absolute positioning. [1]
- **A11Y-9** SC 1.3.3 (A): Instructions must not depend only on shape, colour, size, position, orientation or sound (for example, "the round button on the right").
  Check: axe none; read the UI copy. [1]
- **A11Y-10** SC 1.3.4 (AA): Support both portrait and landscape unless one orientation is essential to the task.
  Check: axe `css-orientation-lock` (experimental); search for `screenOrientation.lock`, `UISupportedInterfaceOrientations` and `android:screenOrientation`; rotate the device. [1][4]
- **A11Y-11** SC 1.3.5 (AA): Fields asking for the user's own details (name, email, tel, address...) carry the matching `autocomplete` token.
  Check: axe `autocomplete-valid` checks token validity only; confirm by hand that every personal-data field has one. [1][4]
- **A11Y-12** SC 1.4.1 (A): Never let colour be the sole signal for a state, error or link; add text, an icon or an underline.
  Check: axe `link-in-text-block`; view a greyscale screenshot. [1][4]
- **A11Y-13** SC 1.4.2 (A): Sound that starts by itself and runs more than 3 seconds needs a pause/stop control or a volume control separate from the system volume.
  Check: axe `no-autoplay-audio`; search for `autoplay` without `muted`. [1][4]
- **A11Y-14** SC 1.4.3 (AA): Body text needs at least 4.5:1 contrast; large text (at least 18 pt, or 14 pt bold; roughly 24 px, or 18.5 px bold) needs 3:1. Disabled controls, decoration and logos are exempt.
  Check: axe `color-contrast`; it cannot judge text over images or gradients, so sample those pixels yourself. [1][2][4]
- **A11Y-15** SC 1.4.4 (AA): Text must scale to 200 percent without assistive tech and without anything being cut off or breaking.
  Check: axe `meta-viewport` (flags `user-scalable=no` and `maximum-scale<2`); zoom to 200% and look for clipped or overlapping text. [1][4]
- **A11Y-16** SC 1.4.5 (AA): Render words as live text, not pictures of text, unless the user can restyle the image or the look is essential (logos count as essential).
  Check: axe none; look for images that contain words. [1]
- **A11Y-17** SC 1.4.10 (AA): At 320 CSS px wide (1280 px at 400% zoom) everything still works and scrolls in one direction only; for horizontally scrolling content (vertical text) the measure is a height of 256 CSS px. Maps, data tables, video and toolbars may scroll both ways.
  Check: axe none; set a 320 px viewport and look for sideways scrolling and missing content. [1]
- **A11Y-18** SC 1.4.11 (AA): Control edges, control states, focus rings and graphics needed for understanding need 3:1 contrast against the colours next to them.
  Check: axe none; measure input borders, icon buttons, toggle states and chart lines. [1]
- **A11Y-19** SC 1.4.12 (AA): When a user forces line height 1.5×, paragraph spacing 2×, letter spacing 0.12× and word spacing 0.16× the font size, no text or function may be lost.
  Check: axe `avoid-inline-spacing` (inline `!important` only); inject those values (a text-spacing bookmarklet works) and look for clipping in fixed-height boxes. [1][4]
- **A11Y-20** SC 1.4.13 (AA): Popups shown on hover or focus (tooltips, submenus) close with Esc without moving the pointer or focus, stay open while the pointer moves onto them, and stay until dismissed or no longer relevant.
  Check: axe none; hover, slide the pointer onto the popup, then press Esc. [1]

## Operable (WCAG 2.2 principle 2)

- **A11Y-21** SC 2.1.1 (A): A keyboard alone can reach and run every feature, with no need for timed keypresses.
  Check: axe `scrollable-region-focusable`, `frame-focusable-content`, `server-side-image-map`; put the mouse away and Tab through each flow; search for `onClick` on `div`/`span` lacking `onKeyDown`, `role` or `tabindex`. [1][4]
- **A11Y-22** SC 2.1.2 (A): Focus can always leave a component by keyboard; if leaving needs anything beyond Tab, arrows or Esc, the UI explains how.
  Check: axe none; Tab into embeds, editors and modals and try to get back out. [1]
- **A11Y-23** SC 2.1.4 (A): Shortcuts on a bare printable key (letter, digit, punctuation) can be switched off or remapped, or fire only while their component has focus.
  Check: axe none; search `keydown` handlers that match `e.key` against a printable character with no modifier test. [1]
- **A11Y-24** SC 2.2.1 (A): Each time limit can be switched off, stretched to at least 10× the default, or extended at least 10 times after a warning that leaves at least 20 seconds and needs one simple action. Exempt: real-time events, essential limits, limits over 20 hours.
  Check: axe `meta-refresh`; search for session timeouts, `setTimeout` redirects and countdowns. [1][4]
- **A11Y-25** SC 2.2.2 (A): Motion, blinking or scrolling that starts by itself, runs over 5 seconds and shares the screen with other content gets a pause, stop or hide control; so does auto-updating content.
  Check: axe `blink`, `marquee`; check carousels, tickers and autoplay video for a pause control. [1][4]
- **A11Y-26** SC 2.3.1 (A): Nothing flashes more than 3 times per second unless it stays under the general and red flash thresholds.
  Check: axe none; step through animation and video frame by frame. [1]
- **A11Y-27** SC 2.4.1 (A): Offer a way past blocks that repeat on every page: a skip link, landmarks or headings.
  Check: axe `bypass`; press Tab once after load and look for "Skip to content". [1][4]
- **A11Y-28** SC 2.4.2 (A): Every page has a `<title>` naming its topic or purpose, and an SPA updates it on each route change.
  Check: axe `document-title`; read `document.title` after each client-side navigation. [1][4]
- **A11Y-29** SC 2.4.3 (A): Tab order must keep meaning and operation intact: focus goes into a dialog when it opens and back to its trigger when it closes.
  Check: axe none; Tab through each page; search for `tabindex="[1-9]` (axe `tabindex`, best practice). [1][4]
- **A11Y-30** SC 2.4.4 (A): A link's text, alone or with its surrounding context, tells where it goes; a bare "Read more" with no context fails. Wording beyond the SC: TEXT-26.
  Check: axe `link-name` (catches empty links only); list every link text and look for identical texts leading to different places. [1][4]
- **A11Y-31** SC 2.4.5 (AA): Pages in a set can be found by at least two routes (navigation plus search, a sitemap or links); steps inside a process are exempt.
  Check: axe none; pick a deep page and count the routes to it (navigation, search, sitemap, table of contents, related links); a single route fails. [1]
- **A11Y-32** SC 2.4.6 (AA): Headings and labels say what their section or field is about.
  Check: axe `empty-heading` (best practice); read the heading outline and the field labels. [1][4]
- **A11Y-33** SC 2.4.7 (AA): Every keyboard-operable element shows where focus is.
  Check: axe none; search for `outline: none` or `outline: 0` with no `:focus-visible` style in its place; Tab and screenshot. [1]
- **A11Y-34** SC 2.4.11 (AA): A focused element is never completely covered by the product's own overlays: sticky headers and footers, cookie banners, chat widgets.
  Check: axe none; Tab down a long page with sticky bars; `scroll-padding` is the usual fix. [1]
- **A11Y-35** SC 2.5.1 (A): Pinch, swipe and drawn-path gestures also have a one-pointer alternative such as a button, unless the gesture is the point.
  Check: axe none; search for gesture handlers (`touchmove`, `PinchGesture`, `onSwipe`) and look for a matching button. [1]
- **A11Y-36** SC 2.5.2 (A): Single-pointer actions fire on release, or can be cancelled or undone.
  Check: axe none; search for actions bound to `mousedown`, `pointerdown` or `touchstart`. [1]
- **A11Y-37** SC 2.5.3 (A): The accessible name contains the visible label, ideally at its start; a button showing "Send" must not be named "Submit form".
  Check: axe `label-content-name-mismatch`; compare each `aria-label` with what is on screen. [1][4]
- **A11Y-38** SC 2.5.4 (A): Anything triggered by shaking or tilting also has an on-screen control, and the motion response can be turned off.
  Check: axe none; search for `devicemotion`, `deviceorientation`, `CMMotionManager` and `SensorManager`. [1]
- **A11Y-39** SC 2.5.7 (AA): Anything done by dragging (sortable lists, sliders, kanban) can also be done with single clicks or taps, such as buttons or a menu.
  Check: axe none; search for `draggable`, drag-and-drop libraries and `onDrag`. [1]
- **A11Y-40** SC 2.5.8 (AA): Pointer targets measure at least 24×24 CSS px; a smaller one still passes if a 24 CSS px circle centred on it overlaps no other target or circle, if another control on the page does the same job at full size, if it sits inside running text, if it is browser-drawn and unstyled, or if its size is essential or legally required. Detail: TOUCH-2.
  Check: axe `target-size` (it judges size and spacing only, so look for a full-size twin before reporting). Platform minimums: TOUCH-1. [1][4]

## Understandable (WCAG 2.2 principle 3)

- **A11Y-41** SC 3.1.1 (A): Declare the page language on `<html lang>`. Check: axe `html-has-lang`, `html-lang-valid`. [1][4]
- **A11Y-42** SC 3.1.2 (AA): Wrap foreign-language passages in a `lang` attribute. Check: axe `valid-lang` (validity only); review by hand. [1][4]
- **A11Y-43** SC 3.2.1 (A): Focusing an element alone never navigates, submits, opens a window or moves focus elsewhere.
  Check: axe none; Tab through and watch for anything that fires on focus. [1]
- **A11Y-44** SC 3.2.2 (A): Changing a control's value never triggers such a context change (for example, a select that navigates on `change`) unless the user was warned beforehand.
  Check: axe none; search for `onChange` handlers that navigate or submit. [1]
- **A11Y-45** SC 3.2.3 (AA): Navigation repeated across pages keeps the same relative order.
  Check: axe none; compare the nav across several pages. [1]
- **A11Y-46** SC 3.2.4 (AA): The same function wears the same label and icon on every page.
  Check: axe none; compare labels and icons across pages. [1]
- **A11Y-47** SC 3.2.6 (A): Help that appears on several pages (contact details, contact form, self-help, chatbot) sits in the same relative order each time.
  Check: axe none; compare the pages. [1]
- **A11Y-48** SC 3.3.1 (A): When the system detects an input error, it points to the field and explains the problem in text.
  Check: axe none; submit bad data in the guarded session for client-side errors; for server-side ones fake the 400/422 with `page.route` and `route.fulfill()`, or (disposable only) submit for real; look for text by the field (a red border alone fails), plus `aria-invalid` and `aria-describedby`. [1]
- **A11Y-49** SC 3.3.2 (A): Every input has a label or instructions, including any required format.
  Check: axe `label`, `select-name`, `form-field-multiple-labels`, `label-title-only` (best practice); flag placeholder-only fields by hand. [1][4]
- **A11Y-50** SC 3.3.3 (AA): If the fix for an error is known, the message offers it ("Enter a date like 31/12/2025").
  Check: axe none; trigger each validation error and read it (client-side ones in the guarded session; server-side ones via `page.route` and `route.fulfill()`, or (disposable only) for real). [1]
- **A11Y-51** SC 3.3.4 (AA): Submissions with legal or financial effect, that delete data, or that submit test answers must be undoable, validated with a chance to fix, or reviewed and confirmed before they are final.
  Check: axe none; walk the checkout, delete and transfer flows up to the final confirming step and stop ((disposable only) finish them). [1]
- **A11Y-52** SC 3.3.7 (A): Within one process, data the user already gave is pre-filled or offered for selection rather than asked for again (for example, billing address same as shipping).
  Check: axe none; walk multi-step flows and look for repeated questions. [1]
- **A11Y-53** SC 3.3.8 (AA): Login must not depend on a cognitive function test (recalling a password, a puzzle, retyping a code) unless there is another method, an aid such as password-manager autofill or paste, or the test is recognising objects or the user's own images.
  Check: axe none; search for paste blocking (`onpaste`) and `autocomplete="off"` on password and OTP fields; look for text CAPTCHAs. [1]

## Robust (WCAG 2.2 principle 4)

- **A11Y-54** SC 4.1.2 (A): Each UI component exposes its name and role in code, its states and values can be read and set programmatically, and changes to them are announced.
  Check: axe `button-name`, `link-name`, `input-button-name`, `aria-toggle-field-name`, `aria-command-name`, `aria-input-field-name`, `aria-valid-attr-value`, `aria-required-attr`, `aria-hidden-focus`, `nested-interactive`, `frame-title`; toggle each control and confirm `aria-expanded`, `aria-pressed` and `aria-selected` follow. [1][4]
- **A11Y-55** SC 4.1.3 (AA): Success notes, result counts ("3 results"), progress and errors reach screen readers without taking focus, via `role="status"`, `role="alert"`, `role="log"` or `aria-live`.
  Check: axe none; search toast and snackbar code for a live region that exists in the DOM before text is put into it. [1][3]

## ARIA and keyboard patterns

- **A11Y-56** Prefer a native element that already behaves correctly (`<button>`, `<a href>`, `<select>`, `<details>`, `<dialog>`) over a `<div>` dressed up with ARIA.
  Check: search for `role="button"`, `role="link"`, `role="checkbox"` and `role="tab"` on non-native elements. [5]
- **A11Y-57** Adding a role commits you to building all the keyboard behaviour that role implies; if you will not build it, use native HTML instead (see the APG section "No ARIA is better than Bad ARIA"). Every interactive ARIA widget must work from the keyboard.
  Check: for each custom `role`, try the keys listed in A11Y-60 to A11Y-66. [5][6]
- **A11Y-58** Keep `role="presentation"` and `aria-hidden="true"` off anything focusable and off its ancestors. Check: axe `aria-hidden-focus`, `presentation-role-conflict`. [4][5]
- **A11Y-59** Reserve `menu`/`menubar` for app-style command menus. Site navigation links belong in a disclosure: a `<button aria-expanded>` revealing a list of links.
  Check: search for `role="menu"` inside `<nav>`; where found, verify arrows move between items, Tab leaves, and Esc closes. [8][9][15]
- **A11Y-60** Modal dialog: opening puts focus inside; Tab and Shift+Tab cycle within it; Esc closes it; closing sends focus back to the trigger. It needs `role="dialog"`, `aria-modal="true"`, a label via `aria-labelledby` on the title, and a visible close button. Only claim `aria-modal` when outside content is truly blocked (`inert` or a focus trap) and covered by a backdrop.
  Check: open each modal, Tab past the last control, press Esc, then inspect `document.activeElement`. [7]
- **A11Y-61** Menu/menubar: Tab enters and exits the whole widget rather than stepping through items; arrow keys move between items; Enter or Down Arrow opens a submenu on its first item; Esc closes the menu and puts focus back on its menu button or parent item.
  Check: walk each `role="menu"` with the keyboard. [8]
- **A11Y-62** Disclosure and accordion: Enter and Space toggle the panel; the trigger is a `button` whose `aria-expanded` flips between true and false; accordion triggers sit inside a heading.
  Check: confirm the trigger has `aria-expanded` and that it changes. [13][15]
- **A11Y-63** Tabs: `tablist`/`tab`/`tabpanel`; Tab lands on the active tab, and the next Tab leaves the strip; Left and Right arrows step through tabs and wrap at both ends; Home and End are optional; the active tab carries `aria-selected="true"`. Native Windows controls do not wrap (DESK-10); web tabs follow this pattern.
  Check: use arrows on the tab strip; Tab must not stop on each tab. [10]
- **A11Y-64** Combobox: the input has `role="combobox"`, `aria-expanded`, and `aria-controls` aimed at the popup; Down Arrow opens or enters the popup, Esc closes it, Enter accepts. DOM focus stays in the input while `aria-activedescendant` tracks the highlighted option.
  Check: type, press Down Arrow, then inspect `aria-activedescendant`. [11]
- **A11Y-65** Listbox: Up and Down step through options; Home and End are strongly recommended above 5 options; type-ahead is recommended above 7. Check: walk custom selects with the keyboard. [12]
- **A11Y-66** Alerts and toasts: an alert never grabs focus, and an important one does not vanish on a timer. Use `role="alert"` for urgent news, `role="status"` for routine updates, and an alertdialog only when the user must stop and respond.
  Check: search toast code for auto-dismiss timeouts; see A11Y-55. [3][14]

## Screen reader basics (VoiceOver, TalkBack, NVDA, JAWS)

- **A11Y-67** Screen reader users jump through lists of headings, landmarks, links and fields. Give each page exactly one `main`, place all content in some landmark, label repeated landmarks distinctly, and nest headings logically (one `h1`, no skipped levels).
  Check: axe `landmark-one-main`, `region`, `landmark-unique`, `heading-order`, `page-has-heading-one` (best practice). [4][16]
- **A11Y-68** Each control should announce its name, role and state together ("Mute, toggle button, pressed"); an unnamed icon button is heard only as "button".
  Check: builds on A11Y-54; run a screen reader, Tab through, and note each announcement. [1]
- **A11Y-69** Verify order and announcements with the real screen reader on every target platform (VoiceOver on Apple, TalkBack on Android, NVDA or JAWS on Windows); axe cannot hear speech output, live regions or lost focus.
  Check: run the main flow once per screen reader; on any one of them, a control read without its name, role or state, a status change not spoken, lost focus, or a reading order unlike the visual one is a finding. [1][3]

## Cognitive accessibility (COGA)

- **A11Y-70** Let the title or top heading state what the page is for, and place the key tasks where they are found first.
  Check: from a screenshot alone, can you say what the page does and what to do next? [17]
- **A11Y-71** Follow familiar layouts and hierarchies, keep visuals and control positions consistent, and make controls look clickable.
  Check: compare with platform conventions; look for unlabelled icons and links that look like plain text. Help placement: see A11Y-47. [17]
- **A11Y-72** Write in everyday, literal words, one idea per short sentence, short paragraphs and lists, with jargon and numbers explained.
  Check: scan the copy for idioms, double negatives and undefined terms. [17]
- **A11Y-73** Never make users rely on memory: signing in, signing up and resetting must not demand memorised strings, arithmetic, copying, puzzles or repeated gestures, and later steps show what earlier ones established.
  Check: walk the auth and multi-step flows. [17]
- **A11Y-74** Design forms against mistakes: collect the minimum, mark what is required, accept many formats (phone numbers, dates), offer only valid choices, and propose fixes.
  Check: enter valid data in an unusual format. [17]
- **A11Y-75** Users can step back without losing input (the browser or system Back button works) and can undo errors.
  Check: press Back partway through a form, then return. [17]
- **A11Y-76** Prefer no timeouts and autosave progress; if a limit is unavoidable, say up front how long it is and whether entries will be lost.
  Check: leave a form idle; see A11Y-24. [17]

## Native software and mobile apps (WCAG2ICT)

- **A11Y-77** Native and desktop apps are judged against WCAG with "web page" read as "non-web document or software" and "web content" as "content"; most A/AA criteria then apply unchanged.
  Check: audit apps against A11Y-1 to A11Y-55 with that substitution. [18]
- **A11Y-78** A "CSS pixel" becomes the platform's density-independent unit: pt on iOS and macOS, dp on Android, epx on Windows. So SC 2.5.8 means 24×24 pt, dp or epx, and SC 1.4.10 reflow is tested at a width of 320 of those units.
  Check: read frame and size values in layout code. [18]
- **A11Y-79** SC 2.4.2 for software: where the platform supports window or screen titles, every window or screen has one naming it or its topic or purpose.
  Check: read window and navigation titles, or listen to the title announced on each screen. [18]
- **A11Y-80** SC 1.4.4 for software: text grows to 200%, or to the platform's largest text setting when that is smaller, with nothing lost (iOS Dynamic Type, Android font scale).
  Check: set the system font size to maximum and look for truncation. [18]
- **A11Y-81** SC 4.1.2 for software: name, role and value must reach the platform accessibility API, not just the screen; stock platform controls usually do this already.
  Check: inspect custom-drawn views with the platform accessibility inspector. [18]
- **A11Y-82** SC 2.4.1, 2.4.5, 3.2.3, 3.2.4 and 3.2.6 cover only a "set of software programs", which seldom exists, so they rarely bind a standalone app; consistent navigation and help across screens is still good practice.
  Check: do not log these as failures against a single app. [18]

## Legal context

- **A11Y-83** The European Accessibility Act (Directive (EU) 2019/882) has been in force since 28 June 2025. In scope: websites and mobile apps, e-commerce, banking, e-books, passenger transport services, computers and operating systems, smartphones, ATMs and ticketing machines. WCAG 2.2 AA failures in these products may carry legal risk in the EU.
  Check: does the product serve EU consumers in a covered category? [19][20]
